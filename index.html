Ethnic Diversity & Heritage

Cultures of Nepal

Explore the unique traditions across Himalayas, Hills, and Terai

Himalayan Culture

Inhabiting the northern high mountains, these communities share deep Tibetan Buddhist roots.

Sherpa Culture

Famous worldwide for mountaineering, the Sherpa people celebrate Lhosar (Tibetan New Year) and perform traditional Dumji mask dances.

Thakali Culture

Originating from the Mustang valley, Thakalis are renowned for their hospitality, unique cuisine (Thakali Khana), and archer heritage.

Hilly Region Cultures

The middle hills host vibrant ethnic groups with rich martial history and artistic traditions.

Gurung & Magar

Known for their bravery in the Gurkha regiment. They celebrate Tamu Lhosar and perform traditional dances like Ghatu and Sorathi.

Tamang Culture

Rich in Buddhist traditions, horse-trading heritage, and distinct musical instruments like the Damphu drum.

Rai & Limbu (Kirat)

Follow Kirat Mundhum traditions, worshipping nature and ancestors with energetic ritual dances like Sakela (Chandi Naach).

Newar Culture of Kathmandu Valley

The indigenous community of Kathmandu, known for architecture, crafts, and grand street festivals.

Art & Festivals

Creators of pagoda architecture, woodcarvings, and metalwork. Famous for chariot festivals like Rato Machhindranath Jatra and Indra Jatra.

Samay Baji & Attire

Famous for Samay Baji (traditional feast). Men wear Haku Patasi style garments or traditional robes during rituals and Lakhey dances.

Terai Plains Culture

The southern plains exhibit rich cultural heritage, unique folk art, and warm festivities.

Maithil Culture

Famed for vibrant Mithila paintings created by women, and the solemn festival of Chhath Puja honoring the Sun God.

Tharu Culture

Indigenous to the Terai forests, known for tribal artwork, stick dances (Lathi Naach), and unique mud-structured architecture.
